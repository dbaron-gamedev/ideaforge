## 1. Executive summary

**Recommendation: greenlight one concept — The Last Interview — and keep three others on file as reserves. Do not fund Borrowed Blade.**

Five concepts were generated against a fixed envelope: a $300,000 CAD budget, five developers, twelve months, Unity, premium PC/Steam, no multiplayer infrastructure. All five are structurally compliant with the brief. The portfolio decision therefore turns on risk, not on fit.

- **The Last Interview (concept-5) is the only "invest" recommendation**, at 70.5/100. It has the lowest production complexity in the set, the highest production-feasibility score (9.0), verified demand from a cluster of 2024–2025 comparables (2,270–5,278 reviews each) and the strongest tag-level revenue medians of the five concepts. Its weaknesses are commercial rather than operational: a capped price point, short-length economics, and a brutal benchmark set led by *Return of the Obra Dinn* (19,002 reviews).
- **Signal Fires (63.5), Hollow Orchard (61.3) and Resonance Ledger (60.9)** are each recommended "consider". Each is a legitimate design with a specific, nameable unresolved constraint — reactive-text volume, launch-window visibility and accessibility respectively — rather than a general weakness.
- **Borrowed Blade (57.7) is recommended "pass".** It carries the highest market-demand score in the set (8.5) and the research confidence is high, but it also has the lowest competitive-opportunity score (3.0) and the lowest production-feasibility score (4.5). *Skul: The Hero Slayer* already occupies the mechanic, and the animation matrix appears to exceed a five-person team.
- **The spread between first and last is 12.8 points** (70.5 to 57.7), and between first and second only 7.0 points. The top four are close; the decision is a judgment call about which risk the studio can best absorb, not a runaway winner.
- **Research confidence is high for concepts 4 and 5 and medium for concepts 1, 2 and 3.** The medium ratings are driven by the absence of a direct comparable for each concept's defining mechanic and, for concept-1, an unsized accessibility constraint. No verified revenue figure exists for any comparable title in any of the five analyses; all demand reasoning rests on review counts, concurrent-player peaks and third-party tag medians.

## 2. Project objectives

The brief sets a narrow, cost-constrained search: find an independent game concept that can be built to a commercially credible standard by a small team inside a fixed year, on a single premium purchase.

| Constraint | Value |
|---|---|
| Platform | PC / Steam |
| Team size | 5 developers |
| Development duration | 12 months |
| Budget | $300,000 CAD |
| Engine | Unity |
| Business model | Premium (one-time purchase) |
| Target market | Global |

**Stated requirements:** generate five distinct concepts; prioritise innovative gameplay mechanics; avoid excessive production complexity; avoid expensive multiplayer infrastructure. Market research was to prioritise data from the last 12 months.

**How the concepts were judged.** `evaluation.json` scores each concept 0–10 on five weighted criteria for a weighted score out of 100:

| Criterion | Weight | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| Market demand | 30% | Is there evidenced appetite for this audience and fantasy? |
| Differentiation | 20% | Is the mechanic distinct from what already exists? |
| Production feasibility | 20% | Can five people build it in twelve months for $300k CAD? |
| Monetization potential | 15% | Does the genre's price and revenue band support a premium launch? |
| Competitive opportunity | 15% | How crowded is the space, and is the timing favourable? |

Demand and feasibility together carry half the weight, which is consistent with a brief whose binding constraints are commercial viability and team capacity.

## 3. Overview of generated concepts

All five are single-player, premium, non-live-service PC titles with no networking requirement — a direct response to the brief's prohibition on expensive multiplayer infrastructure. None requires 3D character animation at scale, voice acting, or procedural 3D environments.

| ID | Title | Genre | Core verb | Stated production complexity | Monetization |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| concept-1 | **Resonance Ledger** | First-person acoustic puzzle / atmospheric exploration | Retune a chamber's acoustics until a tone reaches its receiver | Medium — one wave-propagation simulation, ~30 handcrafted chambers, modular kits | Premium + soundtrack/art-book bundle |
| concept-2 | **Signal Fires** | Turn-based strategy / narrative simulation | Compose a weekly radio broadcast and absorb the misinterpretation | Low to medium — 2D node map, faction-opinion sim, phrase composer, large text corpus | Premium + free second-region update + supporter pack |
| concept-3 | **Hollow Orchard** | Ecology management / systemic simulation | Shape soil, roots and mycelium so an autonomous farmer succeeds | Medium — layered 2D cross-section, graph-based nutrient sim, one autonomous agent | Premium + cosmetic pack + paid expansion season |
| concept-4 | **Borrowed Blade** | Action roguelite / 2.5D platformer | Wear the weapon-form of the enemy you just killed, before it decays | Medium to high — combat feel, animation volume, 8–10 forms × 6 archetypes | Premium + free form update + cosmetic palette pack |
| concept-5 | **The Last Interview** | Narrative deduction / investigative mystery | Cross-reference testimony against documents and file a report | Low — document database, linking UI, twelve static scenes, no VO | Premium + soundtrack / annotated case-file bundle |

**Distinctive design intent, by concept:**

- **Resonance Ledger** makes sound the only interactive material: no inventory, no combat, one verb — listening. The research finds no commercially significant game in which acoustics is the sole mechanic, so the concept would create a category rather than enter one.
- **Signal Fires** removes unit control and the tactical grid entirely; strategy becomes interpretation, timing and reputation, with delayed cascading consequences across a faction graph.
- **Hollow Orchard** inverts the farming fantasy: the player is the ecosystem, not the farmer, and success is measured by what an autonomous character does with the conditions created.
- **Borrowed Blade** makes the moveset temporary and stolen, so combat and traversal are the same act of scavenging rather than the mastery of one build.
- **The Last Interview** has no dialogue options and no branching morality; the mechanic is reading, linking and omitting, and the filed report determines which version of events survives.

## 4. Market trends

### 4.1 Cross-cutting conditions

- **Supply is outrunning the market.** PC revenue is forecast at $45.9B in 2026, up 5.3%, while Steam releases grew roughly 45% year-on-year to late September 2026 (Newzoo via search summary; Steam Page Analyzer). The pie is growing; the number of entrants is growing faster.
- **Narrative was the most productive genre of 2025**, with 51 games passing 1,000 reviews, ahead of Simulation (43) and Horror (39) (Zukowski / VG Insights, Jan 2026). Detective games sit inside that bucket.
- **Roguelite was the #1 tag among 2025's hit PC games**, up 39 positions since 2021, with Roguelike at #2 — but the tag is also among the most saturated.
- **Turn-based strategy is losing share of hits**, down 19 tag positions between 2021 and 2025 (GameDiscoverCo, 25 Aug 2026).
- **Price is compressing.** The median price of hit PC games fell from $19.99 (2021) to $16.99 (2025) in GameDiscoverCo's sample, so premium pricing must be justified by perceived replay value or content density.
- **Tag-level revenue medians vary by a factor of five** across the five concepts' genres (third-party estimates, treated as directional):

### 4.2 Trends relevant to each concept

**Resonance Ledger (puzzle).** The category is large but hostile at the median: 36,696 tagged games, 76% estimated under $5k lifetime revenue, and only 14 of 4,022 puzzle releases in 2025 reached 1,000 reviews (0.34%, against 2.99% across all releases). The sub-genre, however, keeps producing durable mid-size hits — *The Talos Principle 2* (8,701), *Superliminal* (14,656), *Chants of Sennaar* (13,545), *Antichamber* (10,265), *The Witness* (9,167) — and was still being published in April 2025 (*Reawakened*, 1,208 reviews). GameDiscoverCo's October 2026 Next Fest review reports "no major genre trend, just all the existing microtrends playing out in 10-15 different subgenres", with Environmental Puzzles among its tracked categories. The analysis reads this as high category saturation but low design-level saturation: a discoverability problem, not a head-to-head competition problem.

**Signal Fires (narrative strategy).** Narrative is the strongest hit-producing genre, but the specific strategy framing is weakening: turn-based strategy lost 19 positions of hit share, and tag medians for Political Sim (~$1,500) and Text-Based (~$760) sit well below Management. Adjacent 2025 releases performed respectably (*The Séance of Blake Manor* 3,386; *Rue Valley* 1,956). The proven pattern is "systems plus text plus a strong premise" at $20–$30, bought by readers who want consequence modelling — not map painting.

**Hollow Orchard (cozy/ecology).** Cozy is among the five fastest-growing niches: 1,041 cozy releases between 1 January and 27 September 2026 against 428 in the same weeks of 2025 (+143%), with 2,730 unreleased cozy pages and 333 titles dated to launch within 90 days against a "normal" 158. Farming Sim releases grew 37% year-on-year (263 to 361) while its hit rate fell from 20.83% (2024) to 8.3% (2025). Simulation remains the #2 genre by hit count (43 games, 4.1% hit rate, up from 3.76%). The analysis concludes the cozy boom has shifted from audience growth to supply growth.

**Borrowed Blade (action roguelite).** Demand is unambiguous and the data is the best-documented in the set: roguelite is the #1 hit tag, comparables run from *Hades* (143,486 reviews, 54,015 peak) to *Skul* (9,715 reviews, 18,876 peak). Saturation is equally clear: 11,540 tagged games, a 3.5% hit rate below Steam's 4.2% average, releases up 57% year-on-year (957 to 1,505 by 27 September 2026) and 4,856 unreleased pages already live. The platformer framing compounds the problem: 2D Platformer had a 0.18% rate of reaching 1,000 reviews in 2025 (3 of 1,658 releases).

**The Last Interview (deduction).** The genre is in an expansion phase rather than a mature one. Deduction-adjacent tags carry the highest medians of the five concepts' genres (Detective ~$1,600, Investigation ~$1,100), a cluster of 2024–2025 releases all found audiences, and the *Financial Times* framed the moment as a genre birth ("The Roottrees are Dead – and the birth of a new gaming genre", describing "a wave of deduction games"). The corollary risk is that a 2027 launch faces a materially fuller market than the 2024 cohort did.

## 8. Commercial and production risks

### 8.1 Commercial risks

| Concept | Principal commercial risks |
|---|---|
| **The Last Interview** | Benchmark risk is severe and specific — every review compares it to *Obra Dinn* (19,002) and *Golden Idol* (6,652), and *Rise of the Golden Idol* drew only ~39% of the original's volume. Removing dialogue choices forfeits the most marketable narrative tag ("Choices Matter", 19,226 games). Short length and no replay value create refund exposure, and genre price anchors (*Her Story* CDN$12.99, *Golden Idol* CDN$19.99-tier) cap price. The corporate-dissolution setting may read as "office drama" on a store page. The genre is filling up ahead of a 2027 launch. |
| **Signal Fires** | The broadcast comparator peaked at 848 concurrent players — small against the required writing investment. Text-heavy store pages convert poorly; there is no visual hook beyond a hand-drawn map. Comparison to *Suzerain* is unavoidable and unfavourable for a five-person team. Turn-based strategy is losing hit share, so the tag will not aid discovery. A no-combat, no-unit-control strategy game is hard to convey in a 30-second trailer and may be mis-tagged. |

| **Resonance Ledger** | Accessibility is the central commercial risk: a listening-only verb excludes Deaf and hard-of-hearing players and anyone on laptop speakers, and a full visual fallback risks erasing the differentiator — with no data available to size the effect. Puzzle's 0.34% hit rate is the worst category statistic in this evaluation. The design fails three of four of Zukowski's clone-proofing tests. *Soundfall* (997 reviews) suggests "audio" is not itself a demand driver. ### 8.2 Production risks

| Concept | Principal production risks |
|---|---|
| **The Last Interview** | Contradiction logic is QA-heavy: a single false-positive link can break trust in the whole deduction, and the failure is invisible until players hit it. Writing and editing a tightly consistent document set across twelve scenes is the real cost driver for a five-person team in twelve months. Removing VO, 3D and animation also removes production-value signals some narrative buyers use. Technical risk is low; exposure is editorial discipline. |
| **Signal Fires** | Content volume is the binding constraint — coherent delayed consequences require a large reactive text corpus, and under-delivery reads as "shallow". *Suzerain*'s depth was reached over years of post-launch expansion, which a 12-month schedule cannot replicate. Tuning the faction simulation so misinterpretation feels fair rather than arbitrary only surfaces in playtesting. Schedule risk is editorial throughput, not engineering. |
| **Hollow Orchard** | A nutrient/water/mineral simulation risks legibility failure: if players cannot see why the orchard failed, they will read it as randomness. The autonomous farmer must feel intentional rather than arbitrary. Seasonal and pest systems need enough variety to sustain a long-tail management audience within twelve months. Technical risk is low; exposure is design tuning and content breadth. |
| **Resonance Ledger** | A Unity wave-propagation simulation that stays legible and performant on mid-range hardware is a genuine engineering risk for the single systems programmer the concept budgets for all twelve months. If the acoustic model is not readable, players cannot distinguish a wrong solution from a bug — the classic failure mode for simulation puzzles. Procedural audio plus 30 handcrafted chambers is a large authoring load, and content cannot be padded procedurally without breaking puzzle integrity. Schedule exposure is concentrated in one system: a single point of failure. |
| **Borrowed Blade** | Combat feel is make-or-break and requires a specialist combat designer plus an animator inside a five-person team — roughly 40% of headcount before systems or content work begins. Animation volume is the hidden cost (8–10 forms × 6 archetypes × traversal quirks). Without those specialists the game will be compared unfavourably to *Hades* and *Dead Cells* within minutes of any demo, and that comparison is unrecoverable. Handcrafted rooms across four biomes with one boss each adds substantial content load. This is the only concept whose required craft specialisation appears to exceed what the budget can reliably hire and retain. |

## 9. Investment recommendations

### 9.1 Primary recommendation — fund The Last Interview (70.5/100)

The concept is the best match to the brief's hard constraints and the only one whose weakness sits in pricing rather than in execution. Recommended conditions of funding:

- **Hold scope at twelve interview scenes, no VO, no 3D.** The 9.0 feasibility score is a function of that discipline; expanding presentation removes the concept's principal advantage.
- **Price at the genre anchor (~CDN$19.99 tier), not above it.** Genre anchors (*Her Story* CDN$12.99, *Golden Idol* CDN$19.99-tier) cap the ceiling, and short length plus no replay value creates refund exposure if priced higher.
- **Budget explicit QA time for contradiction logic.** This is the identified failure mode and it is invisible until playtesters encounter it.
- **Treat the store page as a design problem, not a marketing afterthought.** The identified risk is that screenshots of prose and menus convert poorly and that "no dialogue choices" forfeits the "Choices Matter" tag.
- **Plan the launch window against a filling genre.** Five notable releases in 2024–2025 plus mainstream press attention means a 2027 launch meets more competition than the 2024 cohort did.

### 9.2 Reserves — hold, do not fund in parallel

The brief's budget supports one production. The following are recommended as sequenced fallbacks, each gated on the specific risk identified:

| Concept | Score | Gate before promotion to "invest" |
|---|---|---|
| Signal Fires | 63.5 | A playable slice demonstrating that delayed cross-faction misinterpretation is legible and fair; a realistic estimate of the reactive text corpus a five-person team can produce in twelve months. |
| Hollow Orchard | 61.3 | A prototype proving the indirect feedback loop reads as intentional rather than random, plus a launch-window plan that survives 333 competing cozy releases in a 90-day window. |
| Resonance Ledger | 60.9 | A solved accessibility design (visual fallback that preserves the differentiator) and a performance/legibility prototype of the wave simulation on mid-range hardware. |

### 9.3 Do not fund — Borrowed Blade (57.7/100)

Demand is the highest in the set and the research confidence is high, but the two heaviest weighted constraints point against it: competitive opportunity is the lowest score in the evaluation (3.0) and production feasibility is also the lowest (4.5). *Skul: The Hero Slayer* already owns the mechanic, so the differentiation upside does not offset execution risk. The evaluation's own guidance is to revisit only with a larger team or a longer schedule.

### 9.4 Judgment versus evidence

The evidence base — review counts, hit rates, tag medians, concurrent peaks — is strongest on supply and saturation and weakest on revenue. The investment recommendation is therefore a judgment about risk concentration: the studio is advised to buy the lowest-execution-risk concept with directly verified demand rather than the highest-differentiation or highest-demand concept. ## 11. Research limitations

The analyses are explicit about what could not be established. These limitations are carried forward unchanged and should temper any confidence in revenue expectations.

### 11.1 Cross-cutting gaps

- **No verified revenue or sales figures exist for any comparable title in any of the five analyses.** All demand reasoning uses review counts, all-time concurrent-player peaks and third-party tag medians as proxies.
- **Tag-level revenue medians come from games-stats.com and are third-party estimates, not Valve data**, and were treated as directional only. 76% of puzzle-tagged games are estimated under $5k lifetime revenue, but that figure is an estimate.
- **Hit-rate statistics use review thresholds as revenue proxies.** Steam Page Analyzer's 3.5% action-roguelike figure and the 1,000-review thresholds throughout are proxies for commercial success, not measured revenue.
- **The Newzoo 2026 Global Games Market Report page returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch.** The figures quoted ($213.9B global 2026, +6.1%; PC $45.9B, +5.3%) were taken from the search-result summary and cross-checked against an executive-summary snippet; they were **not independently verified on-page**.
- **The Financial Times article was paywalled.** Only its headline and summary were seen; it is cited as evidence of mainstream press attention to the deduction genre and for no statistic.
- **One GameDiscoverCo newsletter (October 2026 Next Fest review, dated 17 November 2026) is cited in the analysis but no URL appears in the recorded source list**; it is referenced by title and date only.
- **Audience segments are unsized.** The size of the "audio enthusiast" PC audience (concept-1) and the broadcast-fantasy audience (concept-2) could not be quantified from public data.
- **Accessibility impact is unsized.** No data was found on how many players a listening-only verb would exclude, which is the single largest unquantified element affecting concept-1's addressable market.
- **Mechanic-level saturation is uncounted** for concepts 3 and 4: no data exists on how many games implement indirect control or enemy-form-stealing as a core mechanic.
